# September 19: the first Kestrel transport and reducing handoffs

[History index](README.md)

## Starting point: io_uring alone was not faster

The original single-ring transport performed accept, receive, and send through a native shim, with ordinary Kestrel SslStream above it for TLS. It preserved the real HTTP parser, endpoint, output pipes, and 1,024-byte response. There was no native TLS termination, kTLS, or four-ring sharding.

The first complete TLS comparison measured about 137,936 RPS for stock versus 109,039 for io_uring on keep-alive, a 21% loss. Short connections were about 4,192 versus 3,497 RPS, a 17% loss. Later profiling and repetitions showed a busy single pump and additional per-operation allocation; io_uring was not automatically removing the managed/native scheduling cost.

These early runs used two client threads/cores and 16 connections. They must not be compared directly with the later twelve-client-core wrk2 matrices.

## Completion batching and the first wake-coalescing race

The first optimization collected up to 64 CQEs per batch and avoided writing eventfd for every queued command. Its initial empty-queue decision raced with the consumer: the pump could remove the old last item between a producer's queue check and enqueue, leaving work without the expected wake.

That intermediate implementation stalled. One run averaged about 17k RPS despite active samples around 107k; the entire series was rejected. Synchronizing dequeue with the empty-to-nonempty decision fixed that race, and the verifier was strengthened to detect the misleading low-throughput/liveness pattern.

The final 16-connection TLS keep-alive series was about 113,315 RPS versus stock 121,404. The stock control had drifted from roughly 143k in an earlier series, so the smaller gap cannot be credited entirely to batching. This is the original backend's actual lost-wakeup bug, not the rejected-TLS-accept ownership bug found in the newer backends on September 28.

## Plaintext: immediate sends relieved the single pump

Removing TLS did not remove the deficit. A direct nonblocking send for small output, with real io_uring fallback for would-block/partial output, avoided a queued operation and completion round trip when the send could finish immediately.

An interleaved, same-binary, three-round plaintext keep-alive comparison at 64 connections gave:

| Variant | Median RPS | Allocated bytes/request | Approximate change |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Stock Sockets | 162,057 | 1,640 | Reference |
| Original ring-only send | 113,808 | 2,400 | Control |
| Immediate-send fast path | 172,632 | 2,016 | +51.7% versus ring-only; +6.5% versus stock |

The pump fell from about 89-90% of one core to 58-59%, despite higher throughput. This was a useful scheduling result, but it bypassed io_uring for successful small sends. It is not evidence that an all-ring send was now faster. Short-connection throughput actually regressed about 5.7% against its ring-only control.

## Accept scheduling and normal close were separate bottlenecks

Available connections previously waited for an asynchronous accept operation and continuation before another accept could be posted. An immediate nonblocking accept path removed that forced handoff.

| Plaintext short-connection iteration | Previous custom RPS | Changed custom RPS | Approximate improvement |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Add immediate accept | 9,215 | 17,874 | +94% |
| Add immediate accept and receive | 9,215 | 18,787 | +104%, still 23% behind that batch's stock |
| Normal output drain -> graceful shutdown rather than cancellation-first close | 19,484 | 29,691 | +52.4%; 2.8% above that batch's stock |

The last row is a different matched batch, not another stage of the same 9,215-RPS measurement. Immediate receive remained optional because its keep-alive tradeoff was unfavorable.

Normal closure previously queued cancellation, waited for terminal receive completion, and only later disposed the socket. Initiating shutdown immediately after output drained advanced EOF and removed unnecessary cancellation work. In a controlled 256-connection EOF check, native cancellation requests fell from 257 to one, the listener cancellation. Pins and socket references still remained owned until their terminal completions.

This was the first demonstration that close timing mattered. It did not yet implement the final-send hint or linked send/shutdown added to the newer engine on September 28.

## Reusable completions: allocation and latency, not just RPS

Owner-local reusable `IValueTaskSource` state replaced per-I/O tasks/operations; bounded small-receive completion inlining removed a scheduling hop without running arbitrary application code on the pump. Compact connection setup removed additional per-connection work.

Keep-alive allocation dropped from about 2,016 to 1,632 bytes/request, approximately 19%. Close allocation fell about 962 bytes/request. Against the preceding custom transport, close p50 improved about 10%, single-connection p50 about 19%, and keep-alive/64 p99 about 29%. Keep-alive/64 throughput decreased about 2.4%, so this was explicitly an allocation/latency tradeoff.

Versioned consumption, per-submission identity, cancellation registration disposal, and terminal ownership were retained. These optimizations belong to the older single-ring backend; the later owned-page adapter is a separate implementation.

## Evidence and boundaries

The [original sample report](../../../../samples/NetworkProtoSample/RESULTS.md) contains the complete tables and sections titled `Repetition and one focused optimization`, `Plaintext follow-up`, `Short-lived follow-up`, `Graceful teardown`, and `Allocation and latency`.

Its local `results/` evidence includes `wrk-20260919T093415Z`, the rejected `wrk-20260919T094423Z`, and the `plaintext`, `short-lived`, `teardown`, and `allocations` groups. Their recorded environment timestamps place these phases on September 19.

Real backpressure, partial send, would-block fallback, reset, and EOF checks were preserved. A supplementary HTTPS close-load check after the allocation work failed the strict server-reset warning gate even with the new allocation options disabled. It remained unresolved; no new accepted TLS throughput claim was made from that check.
